The paper crane, *orizuru* in Japanese, is the best-known model in origami. In Japan, a string of a thousand cranes is folded for luck and long life. It is not the quickest model, but every step is one you can learn, and two of them, the square base and the bird base, start hundreds of other models.

Fold it from thin paper: thick paper fights you at the petal fold. Paper coloured on one side works well, and our double-sided paper gives the wings a second colour inside.

## Tips for a neat crane

- **Line up the first creases exactly.** Every later fold depends on them.
- **Crease the petal fold first.** Steps 5 and 6 make all three creases before you lift the point, so the sides know where to go.
- **Leave a gap when you narrow.** Stopping just short of the centre line makes the reverse folds much easier.
- **Stuck?** Look at the next drawing. Knowing the shape you are aiming for usually shows how to get there.
